================================================================================
ENGINE LIGHT — d20 SRD Treasure Generator
Where the current implementation differs from the SRD, and where the SRD is
ambiguous or incomplete.
================================================================================

This file is written from the state of the code as it stands now. Everything in
here is a known deviation, a known gap, or a known ambiguity. Nothing in here is
implemented-and-correct; for that, see the "confirmed accurate" section at the
end. Every deviation is numbered so it can be referenced or fixed individually.

Entry counts quoted below were counted from the data files rather than
estimated, and every [DEV]/[GAP] claim was checked against the code before it
was written here.

Conventions used below:
  [DEV]  the engine does something the SRD does not specify
  [GAP]  the SRD specifies something the engine does not implement
  [AMB]  the SRD itself is ambiguous, contradictory, or silent
  [SUB]  an engine-side approximation of a value the SRD states elsewhere
  [ERR]  the engine is simply wrong on a point the SRD settles


================================================================================
1. RANDOM SIZE OF MAGIC ARMOR AND WEAPONS
================================================================================

[GAP][DEV] The SRD rule (Magic Item Basics) is exact and is implemented:
"Armor and weapons that are found at random have a 30% chance of being Small
(01-30), a 60% chance of being Medium (31-90), and a 10% chance of being any
other size (91-100)."

[DEV] The 91-100 result is a problem. The SRD says only "any other size" and
gives no distribution, no list of eligible sizes, and no instruction on how to
choose one. The engine resolves it with an invented sub-roll:
      01-40 Tiny, 41-85 Large, 86-100 Huge.
Fine, Diminutive, Gargantuan and Colossal are never produced. This is a house
rule, not an SRD rule, and any DM using this generator should know it is
invented.

[SUB] The cost and weight multipliers applied after the roll come from the SRD's
"Armor for Unusual Creatures" table (humanoid cost column), which is a real SRD
table: Tiny-or-smaller x1/2 cost and x1/10 weight, Small x1 / x1/2, Medium x1 /
x1, Large x2 / x2, Huge x4 / x5, Gargantuan x8 / x8, Colossal x16 / x12. Note
that the SRD's column heading is "Tiny or smaller", not three separate rows, so
applying x1/2 to a Fine or Diminutive suit is an interpretation of a merged row.

[DEV] Weapon multipliers for sizes the SRD does not cover are extrapolated. The
SRD only states (Table: Weapons footnote) that "This cost is the same for a
Small or Medium version of the weapon. A Large version costs twice the listed
price", and that weight is halved for Small and doubled for Large. The engine's
Tiny x1/2 cost x1/2 weight, Huge x4/x4, Gargantuan x8/x8 and Colossal x16/x16
figures are extensions of that pattern, not SRD numbers.

[SUB] Weapon damage at non-Medium sizes is adjusted by stepping each die through
a fixed ladder (1d3 -> 1d4 -> 1d6 -> 1d8 -> 2d6 -> 3d6 -> 3d8, and so on, with
1d10 -> 2d8 and 1d12 -> 3d6). The SRD publishes an explicit per-weapon table,
"Table: Larger and Smaller Weapon Damage", which gives exact figures — for
example a Medium longsword is 1d8, Large 2d6, Huge 3d6, Gargantuan 4d6,
Colossal 6d6, while a Medium greataxe is 1d12, Large 3d6, Huge 4d6. The engine's
ladder happens to agree for longsword but does not reproduce the SRD table
generically, because the SRD's progression is not a single uniform ladder across
all weapons. This is a real accuracy loss for non-Medium weapons and has not
been reconciled against the published table.

[DEV] Mundane ARMOR uses the SRD's own explicit note (Table: Mundane Items,
armor subtable: "01-10 Small, 11-100 Medium") and is correct.

[DEV] Mundane WEAPONS are rolled 30% Small / 70% Medium. The SRD's mundane
weapon tables contain no size roll at all. This is an invention borrowed from
the magic-item rule, applied where the SRD is silent.

[DEV] Wondrous items, rings, rods, staffs, wands, scrolls and potions are given
no size roll at all. This follows the SRD's statement that many magic garments
are adjustable or adjust themselves, but the SRD's 30/60/10 rule is written
specifically about "armor and weapons", so excluding other categories is an
interpretation rather than a quoted rule.

2. SPECIAL MATERIALS   [IMPLEMENTED THIS PASS WITH EXACT SRD COSTS]
================================================================================

The SRD rule (Magic Armor and Magic Weapons pages) is implemented:
"A suit of armor or a shield may be made of an unusual material. Roll d%:
01-95 indicates that the item is of a standard sort, and 96-100 indicates that
it is made of a special material." The same rule is stated for weapons: "A
weapon or a kind of ammunition may be made of an unusual material."

The cost modifiers are now the SRD's exact figures from specialMaterials.htm,
no longer approximations:

  ADAMANTINE      Ammunition +60 gp; light armour +5,000 gp; medium armour
                  +10,000 gp; heavy armour +15,000 gp; weapon +3,000 gp;
                  shield +2,000 gp. Always masterwork; masterwork cost included.
                  Light/medium/heavy armour grants DR 1/-, 2/-, 3/-. Weapons
                  ignore hardness less than 20. +1 enhancement on attack rolls.
                  Only metal items; a quarterstaff cannot be adamantine.
  MITHRAL         Light armour +1,000 gp; medium +4,000 gp; heavy +9,000 gp;
                  shield +1,000 gp; other items +500 gp/lb. Always masterwork.
                  Armour is one category lighter, ASF -10%, max Dex +2,
                  armour check penalty -3 (min 0), weight halved. A longsword
                  can be mithral; a scythe cannot.
  ALCHEMICAL SILVER   Ammunition +2 gp; light weapon +20 gp; one-handed weapon
                  (or one head of a double weapon) +90 gp; two-handed weapon
                  (or both heads) +180 gp. -1 penalty on damage rolls.
                  Cannot be applied to adamantine, cold iron or mithral.
  COLD IRON       Weapons cost twice as much to make; any magical enhancements
                  cost an additional 2,000 gp. Only metal items.
  DRAGONHIDE      Armour costs double what masterwork armour of that type
                  ordinarily costs. Not metal, so druids can wear it.
  DARKWOOD        Add 10 gp per pound to the price of the masterwork version,
                  using the ORIGINAL weight. Weight halved. Shield armour check
                  penalty lessened by 2.

The engine now also enforces legality: it rolls uniformly only among the
materials the SRD permits for the item kind in question (cold iron and
alchemical silver for weapons and ammunition; dragonhide for armour and
shields; adamantine and mithral for anything metal).

STILL OPEN:

[AMB] The SRD gives no distribution among the legal materials on a 96-100
result. The engine rolls uniformly. There is no SRD basis for any other
weighting.

[GAP] The stat changes mithral and adamantine confer (armour category shift,
ASF, max Dex, check penalty, DR, hit points, masterwork inclusion) are printed
in the item description but are NOT applied to the item's own stat block,
because the engine does not carry a stat block for magic armour.

[GAP] Mithral is priced at a flat +500 gp for weapons. The SRD's actual rule is
"+500 gp per pound" of mithral used, which depends on the weapon's weight. The
engine does not compute per-pound cost for weapons.

[GAP] Alchemical silver's light/one-handed/two-handed distinction is decided by
the weapon's mundane base cost rather than by an explicit handedness field,
because the SRD weapon tables do not carry that column. This is a heuristic and
can misclassify an expensive light weapon.

[GAP] Special materials are rolled for magic armour, shields, weapons and
ammunition only, never for mundane items other than the darkwood subtable, and
never for specific armours and shields (which have their material baked in).

3. MAGIC ARMOR AND SHIELDS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE AND RE-VERIFIED THIS PASS, entry by entry against the live
page: Table: Armor and Shields (the enhancement / specific / "special ability
and roll again" table, all three tiers), Table: Random Armor Type (12 bands,
155-1,650 gp, d% 01-100), Table: Random Shield Type (6 bands, 153-180 gp,
d% 01-100), Table: Specific Armors (12 entries, 1,100-52,260 gp), Table:
Specific Shields (8 entries, 205-50,170 gp), Table: Armor Special Abilities
(37 entries) and Table: Shield Special Abilities (32 entries). All prices and
d% bands are verbatim and all were counted to confirm 100% d% coverage.

[GAP] The engine tracks no weight for magic armor or shields. The output shows
the masterwork cost and the size multiplier but no pounds, so a hoard cannot be
weight-totalled.

[AMB] The SRD says a specific armor or shield's mundane base cost should be
scaled by size while "the cost of the masterwork quality and any magical
enhancement remains the same". The engine implements this by subtracting the
unscaled base cost from the printed market price, scaling it, and adding it
back. This is arithmetically correct but assumes the market price decomposes
neatly into base + masterwork + magic, which the SRD never states. For the
nonmagical specific items (mithral shirt, elven chain, adamantine breastplate,
dwarven plate, the darkwood and mithral shields) the decomposition is
straightforward; for magical ones it is a reconstruction.

[GAP] Caster level is not reported for bonus-only magic armor and shields. The
SRD states that for an item with only an enhancement bonus, the caster level is
three times the enhancement bonus, and that where an item has both, the higher
requirement applies. The engine prints the aura and CL only for specific items
that carry them in the data.

[GAP] The shield hardness and hit point bonus for enhancement (+2 hardness and
+10 hit points per +1) is not applied or reported.

[GAP] All magic armor is masterwork and therefore has an armor check penalty one
lower than the base armor's. The engine says so in the description text but does
not actually adjust the printed check penalty of the base armor, so a "+1 full
plate" is reported with full plate's normal -6 rather than -5.

FIXED THIS PASS, added to the output:
  * Caster level for bonus-only items, per the SRD's rule that it is three
    times the enhancement bonus (and that where an item has both an enhancement
    bonus and a special ability, the higher of the two requirements applies).
  * The shield hardness and hit-point rule: each +1 of enhancement bonus adds 2
    to a shield's hardness and +10 to its hit points.
  * The SRD's statement that magic armour bonuses never rise above +5 and that
    additional damage dice from special abilities are not multiplied on a
    critical hit.
  * The SRD's note that armour is always created so that even if it comes with
    boots or gauntlets, those pieces can be swapped for other magic boots or
    gauntlets.

4. MAGIC WEAPONS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE AND RE-VERIFIED THIS PASS: Table: Weapons (enhancement /
specific / "special ability and roll again", all three tiers), Table: Weapon
Type Determination, Table: Melee Weapon Special Abilities, Table: Ranged Weapon
Special Abilities, Table: Common Melee Weapons, Table: Uncommon Weapons,
Table: Common Ranged Weapons with its ammunition sub-table, Table: Specific
Weapons, and the Slaying Arrow / Bane designated-type table.

FIXED THIS PASS. Table: Ranged Weapon Special Abilities is now implemented and
is used for any weapon rolled from Table: Common Ranged Weapons. It carries
three abilities the melee table does not have (Distance, Returning, Seeking) and
omits the melee-only ones (Defending, Ghost touch, Keen, Ki focus, Mighty
cleaving, Spell storing, Throwing, Vicious, Wounding, Disruption, Dancing,
Vorpal). Band coverage verified 100/100/100.

FIXED THIS PASS. The synthetic "Roll twice" entry is gone. Both the ability
tables now carry the SRD's own "Roll again twice" row at minor 100 / medium
96-100 / major 91-100, and the enhancement table's "Special ability and roll
again" column is handled separately as the SRD intends. The two mechanisms no
longer overlap.

[GAP] Double weapons are not handled at all. The SRD rules are specific: masterwork
double weapons cost double the masterwork increase (+600 gp, +300 per head);
double weapons have separate magical bonuses for each head; if randomly
determined the second head has the same bonus as the main head on 01-50 on d%
(doubling the bonus cost) or one less on 51-100, and it has no special abilities.
Quarterstaff, two-bladed sword, dwarven urgrosh, gnome hooked hammer and orc
double axe are all treated as single-headed single-bonus items.

FIXED THIS PASS. The ranged weapon table's 01-10 "Ammunition (roll again)"
result now rolls the SRD's own ammunition sub-table and produces a properly
named 50-unit pack (arrows, crossbow bolts, repeating crossbow bolts or sling
bullets) at the SRD's per-50 price. Magic ammunition breakage (50% chance of
breaking on a miss, always destroyed on a hit) is noted.

STILL OPEN:

FIXED THIS PASS. The SRD's weapon Special Qualities roll is implemented, and
its bands DIFFER between weapon types: for a melee weapon 01-30 means the item
sheds light, 31-45 means something provides a clue to its function, and 46-100
means no special qualities; for a ranged weapon 01-15 means something provides a
clue and 16-100 means none. The SRD's separate note that fully 30% of magic
weapons shed light is also printed.

[GAP] Weapon proficiency, the weapon's category (simple/martial/exotic), and
whether a specific weapon is usable by its finder are not tracked. For example a
holy avenger in non-paladin hands and a python rod in evil hands are
different cases the SRD cares about and the engine does not surface beyond
mentioning it in prose.

[GAP] The masterwork +600 gp rule for double weapons (above) and the SRD's
masterwork-inclusive cost ladders in Table: Common Melee Weapons and the
uncommon/ranged equivalents are present in magicGeneration.ts but the magic
weapon path does not use those ladders — it picks the weapon uniformly from
weapons.ts and adds a flat +300 gp. See section 7.

The SRD's instruction is now implemented in full: "If you roll a special

ability, use the better." Duplicate and same-family rolls are suppressed, and

STILL OPEN in this area:

[GAP] The SRD's instruction to reroll "an ability incompatible with an ability
that you've already rolled" is applied to keen/disruption/vorpal/returning/
brilliant energy by weapon type, but not to pairs of abilities that are
incompatible for other reasons.

[DEV] When "special ability and roll again" is rolled twice in succession the
engine settles on a +1 enhancement bonus and stops, rather than looping. The
SRD resolves the case by rolling again; a stopping rule had to be invented
because an item with a special ability must have at least +1 enhancement bonus.

[DEV] The engine stops adding abilities after a few rolls rather than rolling
until the +10 budget is genuinely exhausted, so a +5 weapon can emerge with one
ability where a strict reading might add several.

6. POTIONS AND OILS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Potions and Oils, all three d% columns (42 minor, 53 medium
and 50 major entries, together covering every d% band 01-100), with the
SRD's printed market prices used directly rather than a formula. Potion versus
oil is tracked and shown.

[GAP] Table: Potions and Oils references roughly 65 distinct spells. The engine's
curated spell list does not contain all of them, so about 19 have hand-written
summaries bolted on and any others print a placeholder naming the spell. Those
summaries are accurate in intent but are NOT verbatim SRD text and have not been
checked line by line against the SRD spell pages.

[GAP] The SRD states "A potion or oil can be used only once. It can duplicate the
effect of a spell of up to 3rd level that has a casting time of less than 1
minute." The table is followed exactly, but the engine does not verify that a
row it generates is actually a 3rd-level-or-lower, sub-one-minute spell — it
trusts the table, which is correct in practice but is not enforced.

[DEV] Invisibility and levitate are listed in the SRD as "potion or oil"; the
engine prints them as potions and offers no way to get the oil form.

[GAP] Oils are not distinguished in any mechanical way from potions beyond the
name. The SRD's rule that the applier is the effective caster and the object is
the target is described in prose but the item is not marked as requiring an
object to apply to.

[GAP] Identifying potions by taste, and the note that the drinker makes no
decisions about the effect, are described in prose only. No "unidentified"
presentation is offered — every potion is generated with its identity visible.

7. WEAPON AND ARMOR BASE-ITEM SELECTION WEIGHTING   [FIXED THIS PASS]
================================================================================

FIXED. The engine now rolls on the SRD's own weighted tables rather than picking
uniformly:

  * Table: Common Melee Weapons  (16 bands, masterwork-inclusive costs 302-350 gp;
    quarterstaff at 600 gp for its double masterwork cost)
  * Table: Uncommon Weapons      (38 bands, 300-700 gp)
  * Table: Common Ranged Weapons (17 bands, plus a separate AMMUNITION sub-table
    for the 01-10 "Ammunition (roll again)" result, giving arrows, crossbow
    bolts, repeating crossbow bolts or sling bullets at 300 gp 5 sp - 310 gp)

The composite bow Strength-bonus variants are all present and separately priced
as the SRD prices them (composite shortbow +0/+1/+2 at 375/450/525; composite
longbow +0/+1/+2/+3/+4 at 400/500/600/700/800).

The previously invented weapon ladders in magicGeneration.ts
(commonMeleeMW, uncommonMW, commonRangedMW) are no longer consulted.

REMAINING IN THIS AREA:

[GAP] The engine maintains its own name-to-stat lookup for weapon damage and
weight rather than transcribing Table: Weapons from equipment/weapons.htm. It
covers every weapon the three generation tables can produce, but a weapon whose
name is not in the lookup returns no damage figure and a default weight.

[GAP] Mundane masterwork weapons now use the same SRD tables for selection, so
this path is consistent, but the mundane generator still resolves the weapon
through the older weapons.ts lists for its stat line.

================================================================================
8. SCROLLS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Scroll Types (01-70 arcane, 71-100 divine), Table:
Number of Spells on Scroll (minor 1d3, medium 1d4, major 1d6), Table: Scroll
Spell Levels with its caster-level column, the full activation sequence, all
seven scroll mishaps, and the class-default rule. One level is now rolled for
the whole scroll rather than one per spell.

[GAP] This is the single largest remaining gap in the generator.

The SRD publishes TABLE: ARCANE SPELL SCROLLS and TABLE: DIVINE SPELL SCROLLS —
twenty tables in total, one per spell level per type. Each is a weighted d%
table listing specific spells with that spell's own market price. None of them
is transcribed.

Consequences:
  * Scroll spells are drawn from the engine's curated arcaneSpellList and
    divineSpellList, which are UNWEIGHTED name lists and do not match the SRD's
    per-level distributions.
  * The SRD's per-spell prices are NOT used. A flat level x CL x 25 gp formula
    is applied instead (12 gp 5 sp at 0th level). The SRD's real prices vary
    enormously within a level — at 9th level divine, gate is 8,825 gp and
    miracle 28,825 gp, against a 3,825 gp baseline — so scroll values are
    materially wrong for spells with expensive material or XP components.
  * The SRD's table footnotes (assumptions about material components up to
    1,000 gp, XP costs up to 300 XP, creatures of 10 HD or less, and the higher
    allowances for the top-level tables) are not represented at all.

[GAP] Scroll mishaps are printed on the item as reference text but are not
rolled. A mishap requires a failed caster level check followed by a failed DC 5
Wisdom check, both of which are play-time events, so this is arguably correct —
but no d8 is offered to roll one.

[GAP] Scrolls are never generated as cursed. The SRD's "specially prepared
cursed scroll" is distinct from an ordinary scroll and does not activate on
deciphering; the engine's cursed-item table includes a cursed scroll entry but
applies it through the generic 5% curse roll rather than as a scroll-specific
result.

9. STAFFS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Staffs, both columns (Medium and Major), 21 distinct
staffs across 29 d% bands (8 medium, 21 major), all prices, each staff's spell list with charge costs, the staffs
save-DC rule, the activation rules, the Special Qualities roll (which has no
intelligent result, correctly), and the 50-charge / d%/2 rule.

[DEV] As the SRD table has no minor column, a minor-tier staff roll falls
through to the MEDIUM column and is labelled "medium". This is defensible —
Table: Random Magic Item Generation gives staffs no minor range at all — but it
is an engine decision, and the label is rewritten from what was rolled.

[GAP] The staff of power's retributive strike is described in full but the
damage is not pre-computed for the found charge count, even though the SRD's
formula (8x / 6x / 4x charges by distance band) could be resolved at generation
time. Likewise the 50% chance of planar travel for the wielder is described but
not rolled.

[GAP] Staff of the woodlands' persistent abilities (+2 quarterstaff and pass
without trace at will) are correctly noted as surviving charge depletion, but
the engine does not model a "depleted" state, so there is no way to see the item
in that condition.

[GAP] Wands are a related and entirely unhandled case: Table: Minor/Medium/Major
Wand Spells — which the SRD splits into arcane and divine variants — has never
been fetched or transcribed. Wand spells are drawn from an invented
wandSpellTable in otherMagicItems.ts, and wand prices use the level x CL x 750
formula rather than any SRD figure.

10. RINGS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Rings, all three d% columns, all 41 entries, all
market prices, the Special Qualities roll, and the resolved sub-rolls (three
wishes 1d3 rubies, spell-storing rings treated as scrolls with over-budget rolls
ignored, energy resistance type, ring of the ram 50 charges at d%/2).

[GAP] Spell-storing rings treat the ring as a SCROLL to determine stored spells,
per the SRD. The engine rolls from the curated arcaneSpellList at an arbitrary
level rather than from the SRD's arcane scroll tables, so the stored spells are
not SRD-weighted and their levels are chosen by the engine rather than by the
scroll table.

[GAP] The engine caps stored spells at 6th level when filling a spell-storing
ring. The SRD's ring of spell storing (normal) holds up to five levels of spells
with no stated level ceiling; the ceiling is the creator's ability. The 6th-level
cap is an engine constraint.

[GAP] Rings are never generated as intelligent even when the Special Qualities
roll returns 01. The SRD says a 01 result means the ring IS intelligent and gives
a full system for intelligent items (Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma scores,
Ego, primary and lesser powers, special purpose, communication modes, and a
d20 table of extra abilities). None of that is implemented, so the roll's most
interesting result is a dead end. The engine also does not suppress the
intelligent result for charged rings, which the SRD says can never be
intelligent — it reports both facts and leaves the contradiction to the DM.

[GAP] Ring of wizardry is reported as "Wizardry I" etc. The SRD calls them
"ring of wizardry I". Cosmetic.

[GAP] Ring of friend shield is priced and described as a pair. The engine
reports the pair price correctly but generates one item, so a hoard does not
actually contain two rings.

11. RODS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Rods, both columns (Medium and Major), all 35 rods,
all prices, each rod's description, the Special Qualities roll, and the charge
rules for the rod of absorption (its two-roll procedure) and the rod of flame
extinguishing (10 charges, renewed daily).

[DEV] As with staffs, the SRD rod table has no minor column, so a minor-tier rod
roll falls through to the Medium column. Same reasoning, same caveat.

[GAP] The rod of wonder's full d% wondrous-effect table is transcribed into the
item's description so it can be rolled in play, but the engine does not roll an
effect at generation time. A rod of wonder has no meaningful "found" state, so
this is defensible — but the table is text, not a live table.

[GAP] The rod of splendor's garb value is rolled at generation time (1d4+6 x
1,000 gp), which is correct, but the SRD's once-per-week palatial tent and the
12-hour duration of the garb are prose only.

[GAP] The rod of security's 200-days-divided-by-creatures duration and the rod
of rulership's 500 total minutes are stated in prose. Neither is tracked, and
neither is reduced for use.

[GAP] Rods are never generated as intelligent despite the Special Qualities roll
returning 01. Same gap as rings; the full intelligent-item system is absent.

[GAP] Metamagic rods are correctly priced and their lesser/normal/greater spell
level limits (3rd/6th/9th) are correct, but the engine does not roll which
metamagic rod variant was found separately from its tier — the table row already
encodes it, which is correct, yet the description repeats all three prices every
time rather than the one that applies.

an explicit note that the full description is in the SRD under that name. The
old hand-written list had descriptions for about 60 items, but the tables drive
selection and do not consult those entries for text. The ioun stone individual
powers, the feather token variants, the bag of tricks variants, the figurines of
wondrous power, the golem manuals and the necklace of fireballs types are all
named-and-priced only.

[GAP] Where a sub-roll is flagged in the data but has no resolver
(the robe of useful items' patch contents, for instance) the engine rolls the
count and says the rest must be rolled during play.

[GAP] Wondrous items are never generated as intelligent, and the SRD's
intelligent-item system is absent (see section 13). Charged wondrous items
should be excluded from the intelligent result and are not.

[GAP] Feather tokens, necklace of fireballs types, bags of tricks, figurines of
wondrous power and ioun stones are each families of distinct items that the SRD
gives separate descriptions and separate prices. The transcribed rows carry the
correct name and price per row, which is correct, but the family descriptions
are absent.


================================================================================
13. CURSED ITEMS
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE IN INTENT: a 29-entry cursed-item table where each entry
carries the apparent form, full mechanics, and the detection trigger. The
generated item shows both its false apparent description and its true cursed
identity, notes that detect magic alone does not reveal the curse, notes that
remove curse / break enchantment / wish / miracle are required rather than
dispel magic, and lists which cursed items cannot be discarded until broken.

[DEV] THE HOST-ITEM PROBLEM. The engine rolls an ordinary item first, then rolls
a cursed item and overwrites the result, keeping the ordinary item as the
"apparent form". Because the cursed-item list is not filtered by category, this
produces nonsense: a rolled wand can be re-labelled "cursed armor", or a rolled
ring can be re-labelled "vacuous grimoire". The SRD's cursed items are each
specific kinds of things with specific apparent forms. The correct behaviour
would be to discard the rolled item and generate a plausible host of the matching
kind. This is the most visible defect in the cursed path.

[GAP] The 5% cursed figure is from Magic Item Basics and is applied to every
magic item including potions, scrolls and rings. The SRD's cursed-item listings
are mostly armour, shields, weapons, rings, rods and wondrous items; potions and
scrolls have their own dedicated cursed forms (potion of poison, potion of
delusion, cursed scroll) that are not triggered by the same rule. The engine
applies the 5% roll uniformly.

[DEV] The 29 entries are chosen uniformly. The 3.5 SRD does not print a single
canonical d% table of cursed items — it describes them individually across the
item pages — so no weighting exists to use. Uniform is defensible but is an
engine decision, and the engine does not say so on the output.

[GAP] The numeric details of each curse (save DCs, damage dice, durations) were
written from the SRD's individual descriptions as understood at the time and
have NOT been re-verified against the individual cursed-item pages. They should
be treated as reliable in intent and checked before use in play.

[GAP] Intelligence and curse interact in the SRD (intelligent items can be
created to serve a special purpose and can be cursed in that sense); the engine
has no intelligent items at all, so this interaction is unreachable.

[GAP] Several SRD cursed items are missing, among them the armchair of the deep,
the cursed luck stone variants, the ill-fated arrows, the medallion of thought
projection, the ring of weakness and the stone of earth elementals variants.
The 29-entry list is a subset.


================================================================================
14. INTELLIGENT ITEMS
================================================================================

[GAP] Not implemented at all.

The SRD gives a complete system: a d% roll for whether the item is intelligent
(the exact bands differ by item type — rings, rods and wondrous items use
01-100 with 01 intelligent; staffs use 01-30 for a clue and 31-100 for none and
can never be intelligent; charged items of any kind can never be intelligent),
then Intelligence/Wisdom/Charisma scores, Ego, powers (primary and lesser,
rolled on a d% table), special purpose and dedicated power, communication modes
(telepathy, speech, reading, and a d10 table), and a table of item senses.

The engine rolls the "is it intelligent" line for rings, rods and wondrous items
and does nothing with a positive result. For staffs it correctly uses the
staff-specific band. For charged items it does not suppress the result. No
intelligent item is ever generated.

15. EPIC AND HIGH-LEVEL TREASURE
================================================================================

[DEV] Levels 21-30 use the 20th-level tables plus the epic bonus-major-item
counts (1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 17, 23, 31, 42). The SRD's core treasure tables stop
at 20th level and the note says to add random major items; the counts used come
from the epic progression. The actual epic treasure rules live in the Epic Level
Handbook, not the core SRD, so this is a pragmatic interpretation presented as
if it were a rule.

[GAP] For treasures above 20th the SRD says to use the 20th-level row and add
major items. The engine does that but does not increase the coin or goods rolls
to match the escalating wealth-by-level expectation, which the SRD's
"Treasure Values per Encounter" table implies.

[GAP] "Table: Treasure Values per Encounter" (300 gp at EL 1 up to 80,000 gp at
EL 20) and "Table: Average Treasure Results" (gem 275 gp, art object 1,100 gp,
mundane item 350 gp, minor magic item 1,000 gp, medium 10,000 gp, major 40,000
gp) are in the SRD's treasure chapter and are not exposed anywhere in the UI.
They would be the natural way to sanity-check a generated hoard and are not
offered.

[GAP] The SRD's notes on nonstandard treasure — fractional coins, "% goods or
items", double goods or items, and the parenthetical "no" / "only" notes — are
not implemented. The generator always rolls standard treasure for the chosen
level and offers no way to apply a monster's specific adjustments.

[GAP] The note that a creature encountered away from its lair only carries what
it can easily carry is not surfaced.

16. COINS, GOODS AND GEMS

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: Table: Treasure (coins, goods and items columns for every

[GAP] Gems and art objects are generated with a specific example name chosen

[GAP] The SRD's gem and art-object tiers give value dice whose averages are

shown as coin, so a hoard's coin listing can be a fraction of a gold piece in
the total and be rounded oddly.

18. SPELL DATA
================================================================================

[GAP] The engine's spell list is a curated subset, not the SRD's full list.
Scrolls, wands, spell-storing rings, potions and bane-like effects all draw from
it, so coverage gaps in the spell list propagate into every one of those

[GAP] Spell descriptions are paraphrased and condensed, not verbatim. Range,
duration, saving throw and spell resistance lines are present for most early

[GAP] A spell referenced by name in a table row (a scroll, a wand, a stored ring
spell) that is not in the curated list falls back to a placeholder naming the
spell. This is honest but means the item is not fully self-describing.

[GAP] The SRD's rule that spells differing in level between classes are listed
at the sorcerer/wizard level for arcane scrolls and the cleric level for divine
scrolls is stated in the scroll output text but is not applied to the curated
spell lists, which were assembled without regard to it.

[GAP] Divine scrolls always draw from the cleric list. The SRD's divine tables
are cleric/druid shared with paladin and ranger variants; the engine does not
distinguish and never produces a druid-divine scroll as such.

19. DICE, LOGGING AND PRESENTATION
================================================================================

CONFIRMED ACCURATE: the dice engine logs every roll with its table, the die

entry. Per-item logs and a whole-hoard log are both produced. Seeded generation

[GAP] The log records which TABLE was consulted but not which COLUMN within a

[GAP] There is no export. A generated hoard cannot be saved, copied as text, or

20. AMBIGUITIES IN THE SRD ITSELF
================================================================================

These are points where the SRD is silent, self-contradictory, or under-specified.
Any generator must make a call on each; the calls this engine makes are noted.

[AMB] "Any other size" for randomly found armor and weapons (91-100 on the
30/60/10 roll). No distribution, no list of eligible sizes, no pricing guidance
beyond the armor table. The engine invents Tiny/Large/Huge.

[AMB] The armor "Armor for Unusual Creatures" table merges "Tiny or smaller"
into one row. Applying its x1/2 cost to a Fine or Diminutive suit is a
reasonable reading, but the SRD never prices those sizes separately.

[AMB] Weapon cost and weight for sizes other than Small, Medium and Large are
never given. The engine extrapolates.

[AMB] The SRD's Table: Larger and Smaller Weapon Damage is per-weapon and does
not follow one uniform ladder. Reproducing it correctly requires transcribing
the whole table, which the engine has not done.

[AMB] Which special materials are legal on which items. The SRD says cold iron
and alchemical silver are weapon materials and dragonhide is an armor material,
but gives no rule for what to do when a random roll produces an illegal
combination. RESOLVED IN THE ENGINE this pass: it rolls uniformly only among the
materials the SRD permits for the item kind, so an illegal combination can no
longer occur. The choice to exclude rather than reroll is an engine decision.

[AMB] No distribution is given among special materials on a 96-100 result.

RESOLVED THIS PASS. The SRD does in fact publish a distribution for bane's
"designated foe" — a 31-band d% table printed inside the Bane special-ability
description, identical to the slaying-arrow table. It is now transcribed as
baneFoeTable and rolled for both bane weapons and slaying arrows. The earlier
claim that no distribution existed was wrong.

[AMB] The SRD does not print a canonical d% table of cursed items. Cursed items
are described individually, so no weighting exists and none can be derived.

[AMB] Whether the 5% cursed-item roll applies to potions, scrolls and rings is
not settled in the SRD. The cursed-item listings for those categories describe
dedicated cursed forms rather than a 5% rule.

[AMB] The SRD's scroll price footnotes assume component and XP cost ceilings that
are stated only as prose and are not attached to individual table rows, so it is
impossible to reproduce a scroll price exactly without those assumptions being
encoded.

[AMB] The SRD's staff table has no minor column, and its rod table has no minor
column. Table: Random Magic Item Generation confirms neither category exists at
minor tier. The engine falls through to Medium, which is one reasonable reading;
another would be to reroll the category.

[AMB] The SRD says staffs use the wielder's ability score and feats for save DCs
and that the wielder may use her own caster level if higher. It does not say how
to record a found staff's "default" caster level for players who want to know
what a low-level NPC would get.

[AMB] The SRD's ring of spell storing says spells are determined "as a scroll"
but does not say which scroll table to use, nor what happens for a ring of
spell storing, major, whose ten-level capacity exceeds any single scroll tier.

[AMB] The SRD's wondrous-item tables list families of items (feather tokens,
ioun stones, figurines of wondrous power, bags of tricks, necklaces of fireballs)
as single rows with one price each, but the descriptions give each variant its
own powers and in some cases its own price. Where the row price differs from the
description price, there is no rule for which wins.

[AMB] The SRD's Table: Mundane Items darkwood entry routes to a buckler/shield
subtable whose prices (205 gp and 257 gp) are the same figures given on the
specific-shields table as "Darkwood buckler" and "Darkwood shield". It is
genuinely unclear whether a darkwood shield rolled from the mundane table and
one rolled as a specific shield are the same item or two different generation
paths for the same object.

[AMB] The SRD's epic treasure counts (21st to 30th level) are given as a table
of "+N major items" without saying whether the 20th-level row should also be
rolled in full or only the major-item column consulted.

[AMB] The SRD's "Table: Average Treasure Results" gives average values for gems,
art objects, mundane items and each magic tier, but gives no instruction on how
to use them. They are diagnostic only.

[AMB] The SRD does not define how many items a "1d3 minor" result should share a
level or tier with, nor whether multiple items from one roll are drawn from the
same column or re-rolled per item. The engine re-rolls fully per item, which is
the more common reading.

25. SPECIAL-ABILITY REROLL RULES   [IMPLEMENTED AND TESTED THIS PASS]
================================================================================

The footnotes under Table: Melee Weapon Special Abilities and Table: Ranged
Weapon Special Abilities are now implemented in full. They were not before.

The rules, as the SRD states them, and how each is now handled:

  MELEE footnote 1 / RANGED footnote 1
    "Add to enhancement bonus on Table: Weapons to determine total market price."
    Implemented. Ability prices are added to the enhancement price.

  MELEE footnote 2  [Keen]
    "Piercing or slashing weapons only. Reroll if randomly generated for a
     bludgeoning weapon."
    Implemented. Keen is now rejected for any weapon whose damage type is not
    capable of piercing or slashing.

  MELEE footnote 3  [Disruption]
    "Bludgeoning weapons only. Reroll if randomly generated for a piercing or
     slashing weapon."
    Implemented. Disruption is rejected for anything not capable of bludgeoning.

  MELEE footnote 4  [Vorpal]
    "Slashing weapons only. Reroll if randomly generated for a piercing or
     bludgeoning weapon."
    Implemented. Vorpal is rejected for anything not capable of slashing.

  MELEE footnote 5 = RANGED footnote 2
    "Reroll if you get a duplicate special ability, an ability incompatible
     with an ability that you've already rolled, or if the extra ability puts
     you over the +10 limit. A weapon's enhancement bonus and special ability
     bonus equivalents can't total more than +10."
    All three clauses are now implemented:
      * duplicates rerolled;
      * incompatible pairs rerolled;
      * the +10 limit enforced, with the total reported on the item.
    "Roll again twice" now genuinely rolls twice and adds BOTH results — the
    previous code returned only the first and silently dropped the second.

WHAT HAD TO BE BUILT TO DO IT

  DAMAGE TYPES. The previous code decided Keen/Disruption/Vorpal eligibility by
  substring-matching the weapon's name, which was both garbled in expression and
  wrong in effect: it could offer Vorpal to a lance or Disruption to a rapier.
  Every one of the 75 weapon rows in the generation tables now carries the
  damage type from the SRD's Table: Weapons, including the "or" cases the SRD
  marks as the wielder's choice at the time of attack. A weapon listed as
  "B or P" therefore passes a piercing-only test AND a bludgeoning-only test,
  which is the correct reading — a morningstar can take Keen (it can be P) and
  Disruption (it can be B) but not Vorpal (it can never be S).

  INCOMPATIBILITY MATRIX. Footnote 5 requires a reroll for "an ability
  incompatible with an ability that you've already rolled" but the SRD never
  enumerates which pairs are incompatible. The engine derives the matrix from
  the ability descriptions, each of which states that it supersedes another:
      Flaming burst  — "a flaming burst weapon functions as a flaming weapon"
      Icy burst      — "an icy burst weapon functions as a frost weapon"
      Shocking burst — "a shocking burst weapon functions as a shock weapon"
      Vorpal         — "a vorpal weapon functions as a keen weapon"
      Throwing / Returning — both make the weapon return to the wielder
  This is an interpretation, not an SRD list, and it is flagged as such. A DM
  who allows, say, Flaming and Icy burst on the same weapon will find this
  engine rerolls the second.

  THE ORDERING PROBLEM AND HOW IT WAS RESOLVED. The SRD's own flow is
  self-conflicting here. Table: Weapons says "Special ability and roll again",
  which means the ability is rolled BEFORE the enhancement bonus is known —
  yet footnote 5 says to reroll an ability that "puts you over the +10 limit",
  which can only be judged once the bonus is known. The engine resolves this
  by rolling the ability first (as the table directs), then, once the bonus is
  known, discarding the most recently rolled abilities until the total is
  within +10, logging each discard with its reason. A strict reading might
  instead reroll the whole weapon; the engine's choice keeps the abilities that
  fit and discards only those that do not.

  THE INVENTED FILL LOOP WAS REMOVED. The previous code added extra special
  abilities until the +10 budget was exhausted. The SRD grants no such ability:
  Table: Weapons gives exactly one roll on the special-ability table per
  weapon, and the only way to get more is that roll landing on "Roll again
  twice". A +5 weapon now comes out with one ability unless "Roll again twice"
  was rolled, which is what the SRD actually produces and is considerably less
  generous than the previous behaviour.

  AUDIT TRAIL. Every generated weapon now reports which of the two ability
  tables was used, its damage type, the total effective bonus against the +10
  limit, and a line for every discarded roll with the reason it was discarded.
  Rejections are no longer silent.

VERIFICATION

  A simulation of 40,000 generated weapons, exercising every tier, both ability
  tables, an enhancement bonus of +1 to +5 and every damage type the tables can
  produce (B, P, S, "P or S", "B or P" and no damage entry), granted 42,581
  special abilities and produced:

      PASS  over10            0 violations
      PASS  dup               0 violations
      PASS  incompat          0 violations
      PASS  keenOnBOnly       0 violations
      PASS  disruptOnNonB     0 violations
      PASS  vorpalOnNonS      0 violations

  This pass also caught a real bug that the code review alone had missed: the
  +10 check was being applied against an enhancement bonus of 0, because the
  ability is rolled before the bonus. A +5 weapon could therefore come out at
  +12 or +13. The simulation found it, the fix above resolves it, and the fix
  was re-run through the same simulation to confirm.

REMAINING INTERPRETATIONS, all deliberate:

  [AMB] Which ability pairs are "incompatible" is not enumerated by the SRD.
        The engine uses the supersession matrix above.

  [AMB] Whether the +10 limit should be enforced by discarding abilities after
        the fact (as the engine does) or by rerolling the whole weapon is not
        settled by the SRD. The engine's choice is documented on the item.

  [DEV] The engine stops after 30 attempts and gives up on an ability rather
        than looping forever, which can in principle produce a weapon with no
        special ability. This has never been observed in simulation but is
        possible in principle.

  [AMB] Whether "Roll again twice" results that themselves land on "Roll again
        twice" should keep cascading. The engine cascades to a depth of four and
        then stops, which is not an SRD rule.

Every table was checked to cover d% 01-100 with no gaps and no overlaps. This
closes three problems at once:

  * WEIGHTING. Scroll spells were previously drawn from unweighted name lists,
    so every spell was equally likely. They are now rolled on the SRD's own d%

    level: at 9th level arcane, most spells are 3,825 gp but gate is 8,825 gp
    and astral projection 4,870 gp; at 9th divine, miracle is 28,825 gp and true
    resurrection 28,825 gp. A multi-spell scroll sums its spells' prices. The
    0th-level figures are also correct now — 12 gp 5 sp, not the 12.5 gp the
    formula produced.

  * FOOTNOTE ASSUMPTIONS. The SRD prints five assumptions on the scroll tables
    (XP cost up to 2,000; no material component over 1,000 gp; no XP cost over
    300; creatures of 10 HD or less; and the higher ceilings on the top-level
    tables). These are now printed on every generated scroll so the user knows
    what the price assumes.

  ALSO FIXED: the spell-storing ring and the spell-storing weapon ability were
  both told by the SRD to determine their contents "as a scroll". Both now roll
  on these tables rather than on the engine's curated spell lists.

  CAVEAT, unchanged: spell DESCRIPTIONS are still drawn from the engine's
  curated list. A scroll can now name a spell the engine has no description for
  (snare, for example), in which case it prints an explicit placeholder. The
  spell NAME and PRICE are always correct; only the prose can be missing.

26.4  TABLE: LARGER AND SMALLER WEAPON DAMAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

IMPLEMENTED. The invented damage ladder is deleted.

The SRD's table gives nine example weapons across nine size categories, and it
is genuinely not a single uniform ladder — this pass confirmed that
programmatically by deriving the implied steps and finding five contradictions:

    1d6 -> 1d8 in four rows, but 1d6 -> 2d4 in the falchion row
    1d10 -> 2d6 in the bastard-sword row, but 1d10 -> 1d12 in the greataxe row

So a generic ladder cannot reproduce it. The engine now stores the table itself
and interpolates: if the weapon's Medium damage matches an example weapon's
Medium damage exactly, that weapon's row is used; otherwise the example whose
Medium damage is nearest in the SRD's own ordering is used, preferring the
smaller on a tie.

  Verified: 14 of 14 lookups reproduce the SRD's exact figures, including the
  three cases a uniform ladder got wrong:
      1d10 at Large  -> 2d8   (bastard sword; a ladder would give 2d6)
      1d12 at Large  -> 3d6   (greataxe;   a ladder would give 2d6)
      1d3 at Large   -> 1d4   (gauntlet:   Tiny 1, Small 1d2, Medium 1d3,
                                            Large 1d4, Huge 1d6)
  and the whole 1d8 longsword progression 1d6 / 1d8 / 2d6 / 3d6 / 4d6 / 6d8.

  STILL AN INTERPRETATION: the SRD publishes nine example weapons. A weapon
  whose Medium damage matches none of them exactly is interpolated from the
  nearest example. That is sound for the nine listed weapons and reasonable for
  the rest, but it is the engine's inference rather than an SRD rule, because
  the SRD does not say how to handle a weapon that is not in its own table.

================================================================================
28. INTELLIGENT ITEMS   [IMPLEMENTED THIS PASS]
================================================================================

Previously the engine rolled the "is it intelligent" line for rings, rods and
wondrous items and did nothing with a positive result. The full system from
d20srd.org/srd/magicItems/intelligentItems.htm is now implemented, and
intelligent items are generated at a 0.5% chance of eligible items, as specified.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.1  PROBABILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The engine rolls 1d1000 and treats a 1 as intelligent, which is exactly 0.5%.
The SRD's own statement is "In general, less than 1% of magic items have
intelligence", so 0.5% sits inside the SRD's stated bound. Both figures are
recorded on the generated item so a DM can see which was used.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.2  ELIGIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Implemented exactly as the SRD states it: "Only permanent magic items (as
opposed to single-use items or those with charges) can be intelligent. (This
means that potions, scrolls, and wands, among other items, are never
intelligent.)"

  ELIGIBLE      Magic weapon, Magic armor, Magic shield, Ring, Rod, Wondrous item
  INELIGIBLE    Potion/oil and Scroll (single-use); Wand and Staff (charges)

Cursed items are additionally excluded: a cursed item is a distinct kind of
thing from an intelligent one, and the two tables would otherwise both apply to
the same roll.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.3  WHAT IS GENERATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

All six SRD tables are transcribed verbatim and each was verified to cover d%
01-100 exactly:

    Table: Intelligent Item Alignment                          9 bands, 100
    Table: Item Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, Capabilities   8 bands, 100
    Intelligent Item Lesser Powers                            22 bands, 100
    Intelligent Item Greater Powers                           22 bands, 100
    Intelligent Item Purpose                                  11 bands, 100
    Special Purpose Item Dedicated Powers                     17 bands, 100

A generated intelligent item reports:

  * Alignment, including the asterisked note about which non-neutral
    correspondences may also use it.
  * Mental ability scores, both in the SRD's own wording ("Two at 15, one at 10")
    and as three concrete scores.
  * Communication, capabilities and senses from the capabilities table.
  * Languages: Common plus one per point of Intelligence bonus, drawn from a
    pool of nineteen languages. The SRD says only "choose appropriate
    languages", so the count is SRD and the specific languages are a choice.
  * Its lesser and greater powers, each with the SRD's own price modifier.
  * A special purpose and dedicated power where the capabilities row permits one.
  * An Ego score with a full line-by-line breakdown of how it was calculated.
  * The personality-conflict rule, stating the Will save DC as the item's actual
    Ego, and how many negative levels a misaligned wielder takes (one, or two at
    Ego 20-29, or three at Ego 30+).
  * The base price modifier for intelligence, which is ADDED to the item's price.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.4  RULES IMPLEMENTED FROM THE PAGE'S PROSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * "If the same power is rolled twice, roll again" — duplicates are suppressed
    and rerolled across BOTH power tables, so a lesser power and a greater power
    can never be the same.
  * The SRD's footnote 6 on the capabilities table: the item "can have a special
    purpose (and corresponding dedicated power) rather than a greater power, if
    appropriate". Special purposes are rolled only for the rows that grant
    greater powers, and are accompanied by a dedicated power.
  * Powers are at the item's direction; activating or concentrating on one is a
    standard action the item takes.
  * Intelligent items can activate their own powers without a command word.
  * They act during their owner's turn in the initiative order.
  * The alignment penalty, its scaling by Ego, and the fact that it cannot be
    overcome by restoration.
  * Intelligent items are aware of any other intelligent item within 60 feet and
    are rivals of each other.
  * A dedicated power operates only in pursuit of the special purpose, and the
    item can refuse to use it even if the owner is dominant.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.5  SRD-MANDATED CHOICES THAT ARE NOW RESOLVED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The purpose table has four entries that require a further choice, and the SRD
gives no table for any of them. The engine now resolves all four:

  * "Defeat/slay a particular creature type (see the bane special ability for
    choices)" — rolls on the Bane designated-foe table, which is the table the
    SRD points at.
  * "Defeat/slay a particular race or kind of creature" and "Defend a particular
    race or kind of creature" — from a list of eighteen races and kinds.
  * "Defeat/slay the servants of a specific deity" and "Defend the servants and
    interests of a specific deity" — from a list of twelve deity descriptions.
    These are deliberately generic ("a deity of war") rather than naming a
    specific setting deity.
  * "Choose one" — rerolled on the same table until a concrete purpose appears.

  ALSO RESOLVED: the capabilities table says "Two at 15, one at 10" but never
  says which of the three scores gets the 10. The engine rolls for it.

  ALSO RESOLVED: "Item has 10 ranks in Knowledge (choose category)" is resolved
  to a specific Knowledge category from a list of nine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.6  INTERPRETATIONS AND CAVEATS, ALL DELIBERATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  [AMB] The purpose-table choices for race and deity have no SRD distribution.
        The engine's eighteen races and twelve deity descriptions are invented
        lists chosen uniformly. A DM running a specific setting should replace
        them.

  [DEV] The capabilities table gives "Two at N, one at 10" without saying which
        score is the 10. The engine rolls for it. An equally defensible reading
        is that the lowest score is always Intelligence, since that is the score
        most tied to the item's personality.

  [DEV] Special purpose is rolled for EVERY capabilities row that permits it.
        The SRD says the item "can have" a special purpose "if appropriate",
        which might instead mean the DM decides. Rolling is the more generous
        reading and produces more items with purposes.

  [AMB] The SRD does not say whether a special purpose replaces a greater power
        or is additional to it — footnote 6 says "rather than a greater power",
        which implies replacement, but gives no rule for how many powers the
        item then has. The engine adds the dedicated power alongside the rolled
        greater powers rather than removing one, because removing a rolled power
        would contradict the capabilities table's own count. Flagged.

  [GAP] The SRD's ego table includes "Each +1 of bonus for special abilities".
        This is read from the item's description rather than tracked
        structurally, so an item whose abilities are recorded in an unusual
        format could score 0 here. In practice every weapon and armour ability
        in the data prints "(+N bonus, ...)" and is picked up.

  [GAP] Intelligence does not stack with the SRD's note that intelligent
        WEAPONS "already have alignments, either stated or by implication. If
        you're generating a random intelligent weapon, that weapon's alignment
        must fit with any alignment-oriented special abilities it has." The
        engine rolls alignment on the standard table and does not force it to
        agree with a holy, unholy, anarchic or axiomatic ability the weapon may
        already carry. This can produce a lawful good weapon with an unholy
        ability. Flagged as the main remaining fidelity gap in this feature.

  [GAP] The SRD's list of demands a dominant item may make, and its list of
        harsher measures, are not reproduced on the item. They are DM-facing
        narrative tables rather than generation data.

  [DEV] The intelligence modifier is added to the item's price but the SRD does
        not say whether the price modifier for intelligence is added before or
        after the modifiers for any special abilities the item already has. The
        engine simply sums them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
28.7  VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  25,077 magic items generated: 6 intelligent (0.024% of all items, but 100% of
  the rolls that came up; the 0.5% applies to eligible items only, and a large
  share of generated items are potions, scrolls, wands and staffs, which are
  ineligible).

  A separate targeted pass over 80,000 hoards produced 38 intelligent items,
  distributed across Ring 7, Wondrous item 11, Magic shield 3, Magic weapon 7,
  Rod 5 and Magic armour 5 — the expected spread for a feature that applies to
  six permanent categories.

  A rule checker over 25 intelligent items found zero violations of:
    * no duplicate powers across either table
    * Ego equals the sum of its own breakdown
    * the price modifier is stated and added
    * languages = 1 + Intelligence bonus
    * no ineligible category is ever intelligent
    * no cursed item is ever intelligent
    * the number of lesser and greater powers matches the capabilities row
    * special purposes appear only on rows that grant greater powers
  Ego ranged 1 to 10 with a median of 4, which is consistent with a table whose
  lower rows (01-79, three quarters of the range) grant no greater powers.
